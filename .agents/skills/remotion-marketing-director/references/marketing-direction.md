# Marketing direction

## Choose the job

One asset should have one primary job:

- awareness: make the category, problem, or brand memorable;
- consideration: explain why this approach or product is different;
- conversion: reduce uncertainty and make the next action clear;
- launch: introduce a new product or meaningful change;
- retargeting: resolve a known objection or restore intent;
- product education: demonstrate a workflow, feature, or outcome.

The job determines what earns time. An awareness film can spend time on a memorable world. A conversion ad needs proof, product clarity, and a low-friction action sooner.

## Build the persuasive chain

Choose the shortest honest chain that fits:

- problem -> mechanism -> proof -> action;
- before -> transformation -> after -> proof;
- product action -> benefit -> objection -> evidence -> action;
- surprising result -> how it happened -> product -> proof;
- familiar workflow -> friction -> product replacement -> outcome.

The product should cause the outcome visibly. Avoid a sequence where attractive motion sits beside unrelated benefit copy.

## Proof hierarchy

Prefer evidence the viewer can inspect:

1. Real product behavior or interface action.
2. Verifiable measurement with method and context.
3. Customer evidence with permission and attribution.
4. Product architecture or mechanism that makes the claim plausible.
5. Demonstration under realistic conditions.
6. Brand assertion, used only for positioning rather than proof.

Mark provisional claims in the brief. Do not polish an unsupported claim into apparent certainty. The test is what a line asserts, not how it is phrased: framing and humor may be invented, but names, numbers, capabilities, and quotes must come from the product.

## CTA

The closing action should follow the resolved uncertainty. Specify:

- action: what the viewer should do;
- destination: where the action leads;
- value: what happens after the click or scan;
- friction: price, signup, download, trial, waitlist, or sales contact;
- visual persistence: how long the action remains readable.

## Platform research

Platform guidance is time-sensitive. Recheck the intended placement before production and record the source plus access date.

Current primary-source starting points:

- [TikTok performance ad creative practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices): vertical 9:16, sound, safe zones, early proposition, captions, testing, and creative refresh guidance.
- [Meta Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads): vertical video, audio, safe zones, placement customization, and A/B testing.
- [Google video ad best practices](https://developers.google.com/authorized-buyers/rtb/video_best_practices): delivery, review, duration, error, and view-rate considerations.

Treat platform recommendations as hypotheses and constraints, not universal creative laws. Record performance results separately from aesthetic preference.

## Outcome learning

Creative quality can be reviewed before launch. Campaign performance cannot be guaranteed from the render. Choose a success signal that matches the asset's job and record actual results after delivery.

- Awareness may use qualified reach, early retention, completed views, or measured recall.
- Consideration may use watch depth, product-page engagement, or a downstream intent signal.
- Conversion may use click-through, conversion rate, cost per result, or revenue with the correct attribution caveats.

When comparing variants, change one major creative hypothesis when practical, keep audience and delivery conditions comparable, and record the observation without claiming causality the test cannot support. Feed verified results into the project lesson and library promotion process.

## Missing information that matters

Before final direction, resolve or explicitly mark:

- product or offer accuracy;
- target placement and aspect ratio;
- intended audience and market;
- destination page consistency;
- brand assets and fonts;
- testimonial, music, footage, model, and image rights;
- regulated-category or regional disclosure needs;
- product screenshots, recordings, models, or prototypes;
- success measure and variant comparison plan.
