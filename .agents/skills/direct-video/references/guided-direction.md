# Guided direction

Use this when the user wants a video but does not have filmmaking, editing, motion-design, or advertising vocabulary.

## Start from what the user knows

Collect only information that changes the direction:

- What are we showing, teaching, or selling?
- Who should care, and what do they care about now?
- What should they understand, feel, or do afterward?
- Where will they encounter the video?
- Which product, brand, source, or proof assets already exist?
- What must be true, legally safe, and consistent with the destination page?

Ask one plain-language question at a time only when the answer cannot be inferred safely. If several inputs are missing, begin with the question that changes the core promise. Do not turn the conversation into an intake form.

## Recommend before asking for taste

Translate the request into a concrete recommendation:

```markdown
I recommend a 20-second vertical product story. It opens on the frustrating old workflow, lets the interface replace it in one continuous move, proves the time saved with one real example, and closes on the product in use. The movement should feel precise and dimensional, with one restrained 3D hero moment rather than constant camera motion.
```

Then ask whether that interpretation feels right. If a real decision remains, offer at most two options and explain the visible tradeoff:

- Product-first: the interface appears immediately, which is clearer and faster.
- Story-first: the problem appears before the product, which creates more tension but delays recognition.

Do not ask the user to choose between unlabeled professional techniques.

## Give the user useful control

Invite feedback through things a non-editor can perceive:

- "The opening feels slow."
- "The product should appear earlier."
- "This looks too playful for us."
- "I cannot tell what changed."
- "Keep the glassy depth, but remove the spinning camera."

Translate that reaction into stable beat and shot deltas. Report the intended change before rebuilding.

## Checkpoints

Use three review surfaces for a substantial video:

1. Design board: the approval surface before production, described below.
2. Animated rough cut: complete timing with representative visuals and provisional audio.
3. Final cut: polished assets, audio mix, captions, platform variants, and verification.

Skip a checkpoint when the user has already supplied or approved its decisions. A novice should never have to approve implementation details they cannot reasonably evaluate.

## Design board

Every video of 45 seconds or more, and any shorter one whose look is still open, gets a design board before production. It is one page the person opens in a browser:

- the core claim, hook, and visual thesis in a sentence each;
- palette, type, and motion character;
- one row per beat: its ID, job, duration, and a style frame drawn with the real renderer wherever possible;
- open questions, each with a recommended answer.

Each beat carries a status of proposed, approved, or changes requested. The person approves, comments, or asks for changes in chat by beat ID, and the board and `memory/decisions.md` record the outcome. Production starts only from approved beats. Keep the board current through revisions so it stays the shared picture of the video. Turn the first board into a template in `assets/` once a second one shows what varies.

## Explain the result

At handoff, tell the user:

- what the opening asks the viewer to notice;
- what each major beat accomplishes;
- where the proof appears;
- why the final action follows;
- which parts are easiest to revise or reuse.

Keep the explanation shorter than the direction artifacts.
