# Short-form vertical video

For vertical pieces of 15 to 60 seconds on Reels, Shorts, TikTok and X. These rules start as
candidates. They come from a measured study of eight reels by a technical-explainer account
with about 115,000 followers ([graduate_ml](https://www.instagram.com/graduate_ml/),
2026-10-20), and each becomes firm only once vuhri's own posts support it. Platform sizes,
safe zones and limits live in `manage-social`'s [platform reference](../../manage-social/references/platforms.md).

## Structure

- **Subject in the first frame.** No title card, logo or intro. The first frame already shows
  the thing being explained, and the first spoken line lands within 3 seconds.
- **One continuous canvas.** Each step transforms objects already on screen instead of cutting
  to a new layout. The study's reels contained no hard cuts at all. Cut only when the break is
  the point.
- **One idea, about 35 to 45 seconds.** The study's best performers ran 38 to 40 seconds, and
  its two weakest ran 64 and 91 seconds.
- **A headline band.** A line just inside the top of the safe zone carries the current claim
  or formula and changes with each step, so a viewer with the sound off can follow the argument.
- **Colour means one thing for the whole video.** Fix each colour's role in the beat sheet,
  following the brand's semantics.
- **Make the abstract literal.** Draw the quantity as an object that behaves: a squared error
  as a square that shrinks, a tax as a bite taken out of a coin.
- **A familiar handle.** Open on something the audience already knows from its own world,
  then explain the idea through it.

## Narration

- Short declarative lines of about 3.5 seconds with short gaps. The study measured 160 to 200
  words a minute, which is faster than long-form narration.
- When the argument is a ladder, give every step the same sentence shape, so the rhythm
  carries the structure.
- A quiet continuous bed under the voice, with no silent gaps. Follow
  [audio direction](audio-direction.md) for levels and rights.
- Burn in short captions that follow the voice a phrase at a time, inside the safe zone.
  Reels have no alt text, and many viewers watch muted. Skip them only when the headline band
  already carries every spoken claim.

## Endings

- **Loop.** Rewind or transform back into the opening frame, so an autoplay replay looks
  seamless. Use it by default for explainers. Five of the eight studied reels ended this way.
- **Question.** Ask the viewer to commit to an answer, such as a choice from A to D or a call
  of up, hold or down, and hold the options on screen for at least 3 seconds. Use it when
  comments are the goal. It drew the study's highest comment count by far. Ask for a choice
  and a reason, never for a specific word or emoji, which platforms treat as engagement bait.
- **Avoid sending viewers elsewhere** at the end ("comment a keyword", "full video on YouTube").
  Those were the study's two weakest reels. Put links in the caption or the profile instead.
- A brand sting, if used, lasts 1.5 seconds at most and sits inside the loop, not after it.

## Layout

Keep everything readable inside the platform's safe zone, with the subject in the upper
middle and the bottom third clear, where the caption and buttons sit. Compose vertical
natively, never by cropping a landscape render.
