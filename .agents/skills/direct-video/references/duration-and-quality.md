# Duration and quality

Duration changes the directing problem. Use these bands as workflow limits, then verify current platform constraints separately.

| Duration | Best use | Direction requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 to 6 seconds | Hero loop, logo or product sting, transition, micro-animation | One action, one focal object, seamless or decisive ending |
| 6 to 15 seconds | Bumper, quick ad, hook variant, product reveal | Immediate premise, one proof or benefit, compact brand and action |
| 15 to 30 seconds | Social ad, feature story, launch clip | Hook, mechanism, proof, action; usually four to six beats |
| 30 to 45 seconds | Product film, richer ad, concise explainer | Strong progression, controlled density, planned audio and breathing room |
| 45 to 90 seconds | Mini case study, educational or launch narrative | Sections, motif continuity, multiple proof moments, full narration and caption plan |
| More than 90 seconds | Long-form explainer, demo, lesson, case study | Modular chapters, separate sequence verification, stronger source and continuity management |

## Initial product quality envelope

Treat 2 to 45 seconds as the default high-polish envelope for rapid creation. Work from 45 to 90 seconds is supported, but it needs a stronger script, more review surfaces, and chapter-level verification. Above 90 seconds, route to a long-form workflow rather than promising the same rapid iteration profile.

This is a production boundary, not a claim that long videos cannot be excellent.

## Time budget

Every interval needs an intentional job. A job may be motion, comprehension, anticipation, emphasis, proof, or silence. A hold is valid when it lets the viewer understand or feel something. Dead time is an interval whose removal improves neither meaning nor rhythm.

## Reading time (candidate)

Pace comes from motion and cuts, never from pulling text away early. A line the viewer must read stays fully settled, counted from when the whole line is on screen, for at least 0.8 seconds if it has three words or fewer, otherwise 0.3 seconds per word and at least 1.2 seconds. Bring text in fast, then hold it. If a scene carries more text than its duration allows, cut copy or split the scene. Text that is only texture is exempt. Record each line's settled and exit times as `text` entries in the shot manifest so the verifier flags short holds. These numbers come from the brag project and stay candidate until vuhri's renders confirm them.

## Per-second review

For videos up to 45 seconds, sample at least one frame per second and every shot transition. Review the surrounding motion, not only the still.

Check:

- Can the viewer find the intended focal point immediately?
- Does the visible state differ meaningfully from the previous sample?
- If nothing moves, is the hold earning comprehension or emphasis?
- Are text, narration, product action, and sound competing?
- Is the brand, claim, or CTA readable inside the platform safe zone?
- Does any camera or depth change obscure the product or message?
- Does the shot exit because its job is complete?

For longer work, run the same audit at shot boundaries and inspect dense or high-risk beats at one-second intervals.
