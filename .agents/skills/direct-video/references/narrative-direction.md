# Narrative direction

Use this reference to choose what the video is doing before deciding how it looks.

## Editorial contract

Lock these claims in plain language:

- Who is watching, in what context, and what do they already believe?
- What should change in their understanding, feeling, or action?
- What single sentence should survive a day later?
- Which claims require sources, attribution, uncertainty, or permission?
- What duration, canvas, platform, and audio conditions constrain the piece?

If the user already answered an item, keep their answer. Do not replace a specific direction with a generic archetype.

## Angle

An angle is the chosen relationship between the topic and the viewer. It is more specific than a topic and more durable than a hook.

Useful angle tests:

- The compression test: can someone repeat it in one sentence?
- The consequence test: why does this matter to this viewer now?
- The exclusion test: what tempting adjacent material does this angle leave out?
- The evidence test: can the piece honestly support the promise it makes?

When the angle is open, propose a small set of meaningfully different options. Vary the argument, not just the wording.

## Hook mechanisms

Choose a mechanism because it creates the right initial question. Do not treat hooks as ornamental headlines.

| Mechanism | Opens with | Viewer question | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Incomplete action | An event begins but cannot finish | What is blocking it? | Artificial delay with no payoff |
| Contradiction | A plausible belief meets conflicting evidence | Which belief is wrong? | Sensational claim the body cannot defend |
| Hidden system | A familiar action reveals unseen work | What happens underneath? | Listing complexity without an argument |
| Consequence first | The outcome appears before the cause | How did this happen? | Context withheld too long |
| Direct demonstration | The central phenomenon happens immediately | Why did that work? | Demo is impressive but unrelated |
| Recognition | A precise audience experience appears | Is this about my situation? | Generic pain-point language |
| Scale shift | The view jumps between small and large systems | How are these scales connected? | Visual spectacle without causality |
| Before and after | Two states create an unresolved gap | What changed between them? | Cherry-picked comparison |

Write the hook contract as: `initial state -> disturbance -> question opened -> promised payoff`.

## Narrative movement

Choose the smallest movement that carries the argument:

- trace: follow an entity through a system;
- reveal: expose hidden structure in stages;
- transform: show one state becoming another;
- compare: hold two alternatives against the same criteria;
- escalate: accumulate consequences or complexity;
- diagnose: move from symptom to cause to correction;
- test: make a prediction, run the case, inspect the result;
- resolve: return to the opening question with a changed model.

A video may combine movements, but one should govern the whole piece. Each beat should advance it.

## Visual thesis

A visual thesis describes the meaningful change that can be watched. Examples:

- A packet crosses increasingly explicit control boundaries.
- One tangled set of pairwise links reorganizes into a shared interface.
- Two identical inputs diverge after a hidden gate changes state.
- A large number decomposes into the few forces that produced it.

"Warm technical," "cinematic," and "minimal" are style directions. They do not substitute for a visual thesis.
